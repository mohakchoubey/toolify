Chess History - How, When and where It was invented ?
By Mohak · October 7, 2026

Chess is one of the world's oldest strategy games. Its earliest known form developed in northern India around the 6th century CE, during the Gupta period. It was called chaturanga, a Sanskrit word associated with the four divisions of an army: infantry, cavalry, elephants, and chariots. These military units were represented on the board and formed the basic idea behind several modern chess pieces.

From India, chaturanga gradually spread westward through trade and cultural connections. It reached Persia, where the game became known as chatrang or shatranj. Persian players developed the rules further, and the game became an important part of intellectual and court culture. Some chess terminology used today has roots in Persian traditions.

The game later spread through the Islamic world and reached Europe during the medieval period. It was introduced into Europe through regions such as Spain and Italy, probably through contacts between the Islamic world and European societies. Different countries developed their own versions and rules, so medieval chess was not exactly the same game that we play today.

The biggest transformation happened in Europe during the late 15th century. Several pieces became much more powerful, especially the queen and bishop. The queen changed from a relatively weak piece into the most powerful piece on the board, while the bishop gained greater mobility. These changes made games faster and more tactical and gradually created the foundation of modern chess.

Modern chess eventually developed a standardized set of rules and became an organized international sport. The first official World Chess Championship was played in 1886, when Wilhelm Steinitz defeated Johannes Zukertort. Today, chess is played worldwide both on physical boards and online, but its central idea remains remarkably similar to its ancient Indian predecessor: two players use strategy, calculation, and planning to outmaneuver each other's armies.
